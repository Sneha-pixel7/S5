ALGORITHM: Impact of Feature Scaling on Gradient-Based Classification

PROGRAM OBJECTIVE:
Evaluate the effect of feature standardization (StandardScaler) on convergence stability, classification metrics, and parameter weights in Logistic Regression using the Pima Indians Diabetes dataset.

STEP 1: LOAD & PREPROCESS DATA
    1.1. Load Pima Indians Diabetes CSV dataset (8 medical predictors, 1 binary outcome).
    1.2. Define column names: Pregnancies, Glucose, BloodPressure, SkinThickness, Insulin, BMI, DiabetesPedigree, Age, Outcome.
    1.3. Split into predictors X (8 features) and binary target y ('Outcome').
    1.4. Partition into training (70%) and testing (30%) subsets with random_state=42.

STEP 2: MODEL TRAINING ON UNSTANDARDIZED (RAW) DATA
    2.1. Train LogisticRegression with solver='saga', max_iter=1000 directly on raw X_train.
    2.2. Due to disparity in feature scales (e.g., Insulin in 100s vs DiabetesPedigree < 1), gradient updates oscillate and struggle to converge.
    2.3. Predict outcomes on unscaled X_test and record learned coefficients model_unscaled.coef_[0].

STEP 3: FEATURE STANDARDIZATION & RETRAINING
    3.1. Fit StandardScaler on X_train to compute mean mu and variance sigma^2 per feature:
         x_scaled = (x - mu) / sigma
    3.2. Transform X_train and X_test using fitted parameters.
    3.3. Train LogisticRegression with identical hyperparameters (solver='saga', max_iter=1000) on standardized X_train_scaled.
    3.4. Predict outcomes on standardized X_test_scaled and record learned coefficients model_scaled.coef_[0].

STEP 4: COMPARATIVE METRIC CALCULATION
    4.1. Compute Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score for:
         - Unscaled pipeline
         - Scaled pipeline
    4.2. Tabulate results in a summary DataFrame and display performance gains.

STEP 5: VISUALIZATION
    5.1. Plot side-by-side bar chart of learned weight coefficients for all 8 features.
    5.2. Note that unscaled weights are distorted by raw magnitude, whereas standardized weights faithfully reflect genuine feature influence on the probability of diabetes.
